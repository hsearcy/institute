## 01-combat.png

Use case: ui-mockup.
Asset type: landscape 16:9 game concept art, polished full-screen PC/tablet game screenshot.
Primary request: envision a Diablo-inspired isometric loot RPG for a six-year-old where solving math powers combat. Original fantasy art, atmospheric and adventurous, tactile stylized 3D with painterly finish, chunky readable silhouettes, richly modeled stone and gear. Age-appropriate monsters, no gore or horror. A small brave adventurer wears a teal hooded cape, bronze armor, and carries a glowing amber crystal sword. Environments use deep blue slate, teal crystal lighting, warm amber firelight; UI uses dark carved slate panels, restrained brass edging, crisp large ivory type. Sophisticated game art rather than an educational worksheet or mobile casino. Coherent readable hierarchy, generous touch targets, very little text, no tiny paragraphs, no brand logos or watermarks. All gameplay is turn-based with automatic movement between encounters.
Scene: a beautifully lit ruined underground hall, broken arches, crystals and torchlight. Isometric room occupies the left two thirds; the hero faces one large moss-covered stone guardian with a glowing crystal core. A ready amber spell curls around the sword; the entire encounter visibly waits for the answer. A small monster label says "Moss Guardian" above a three-segment guard meter. Subtle top-left location label "The Sunken Vault". Top-right a compact five-room progress strip with third node active.
Composition: the right third is one clean carved-stone spell panel. Exact panel text: "Your turn", then "Power your strike", then the large equation "8 + 7 = ?", an empty answer field, a large readable 3-column numeric keypad 1 through 9 with 0 centered below, and a broad button "Cast". Below it, two compact readable buttons "Hear it" and "Help me". A small sentence says "Take your time". Minimal bottom-left inventory: sword, shield, closed backpack with handsome item icons. The math is the single main action, clearly connected to the awaiting sword strike, the room remains large and impressive. No countdown, joystick, WASD overlay, daily quests, floating answer choices, or wall of stats. This is a convincing playable-interface concept, not a poster.
