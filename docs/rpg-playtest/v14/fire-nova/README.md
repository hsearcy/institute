# Fire Nova visual evidence

These are actual Three.js renders and isolated local gameplay captures, not concept art. The local profiles use disposable `/tmp` databases with the existing tutor, mastery and reward APIs. No family deployment data was used or changed.

The independent art critic accepted the final actual-loop desktop Fire Nova sequence at **8/10** for both the ordinary 14-enemy cast and the 39-enemy burn/echo cast. This covers visible palm gather, descending strand/floor seed, advancing front, localized contacts, supported collapse, ash erosion and cleanup. All 115 decoded frames in each reviewed range were inspected at 40 ms video spacing, supported by the finer actual-render timing trace; it is not a subframe timing claim. Portrait, landscape and reduced-quality crossing retain their separately reviewed static 8/10 scope, without claiming an unseen mobile motion benchmark.

- `front-release.png`: exact simulation age 0.35 s, native 1440×900, full quality. Four uneven combustion patches with narrow hot channels, cooler gas and short crest fragments.
- `supported-collapse.png`: exact 1.15 s, connected full-scale bodies settling before erosion. The later generic gray stone residue was removed in subsequent HUD captures.
- `desktop-contact.png`, `portrait-contact.png`, `landscape-contact.png`: real tutor/reward solves, manually stepped to 0.65 s for visual inspection. Each solve added 32 XP and advanced to the next question.
- `fallback-crossing-contact.png`: fresh browser, performance quality, real crossing solve. The front, flame volume, ground heat and ash share the actual bridge/chasm floor mask.20GLBs loaded and instrumented image decoding reported no failures. Earlier transient decode warnings in a multi-context run did not reproduce; their cause is unproven.
- `boss-echo.png`: real 48-enemy boss solve at 1.05 s with Twin Nova enabled; 106 XP and 48 kills awarded. This loadout did not have a burn or wide modifier.
- `normal-loop.webm`: ordinary actual-HUD animation after a real answer, no simulation-time overrides. The recorded and separate unrecorded solves both advanced XP, kills and questions without page errors. The captures retain the then-current public hero; the replacement hero is reviewed separately.

## Measurements

`gpu-and-cadence.json` retains raw measurements. On the isolated native Intel GPU at 1440×900, a fixed 0.35 s scene A/B hid or showed only the two Nova-front layers. 30 EXT timer queries per case measured median 10.24 ms / p95 10.35 ms with the front hidden and 13.11 ms / p95 13.31 ms visible. This measures GL work, not browser/OS presentation. A separate renderer-only normal loop had 163 attack frames with median/p95rAF interval 16.7 ms.

`normal-loop.json` retains actual-HUD timing. The recorded cast had 148 attack frames and the separate unrecorded cast 149. Both averaged about 55.2 Hz, with median 16.7 ms and p95 33.3 ms intervals. These are scheduling cadence observations, not GPU timings or a substitute for animation-art review. The encoded video frame rate is not presented as gameplay FPS.

## Review history

Independent review of all 114 decoded normal-loop frames accepted observed front evolution, contact/recoil, supported settling, erosion, cleanup and camera/HUD continuity at 8. The old-hero opening remains 7; a CastOrigin gather/release integration is under review. Echo static composition and Cinderbranch burn/echo fade were accepted at 8, while a combined-modifier normal-loop clip remains pending. The real Cinderbranch boss solve verified `burning=true`, `echo=true`, 48 kills and 106 XP with no bitmap failures; its only missing resource was `/favicon.ico`. The wider Nova renderer option remains supported, but the current shared catalogue does not expose a wide modifier for this spell; it is not represented as a tested player unlock here.

The corrected v26 CastOrigin opening now has independent **8/10 static connection** acceptance. `opening-seed-native.png` shows the native-size .17 s seed; `opening-check.json` records both legacy and authored actors with zero console/WebGL errors and successful room resets. One tapering strand leads from the animated hand into a small irregular floor seed, which joins the unchanged .18 s wave. The invalid v25 seed shader used a reserved GLSL name; it was corrected and is not represented as accepted evidence.

The v27 authored-hero actual-loop check exposes a remaining timing blocker. `authored-cadence-check.json` records approximately 28 Hz both with and without recording (median 33.3 ms, p95 50 ms); `authored-echo-check.json` records 22.6 Hz for the 39-enemy burn/echo solve. These current full-quality scenes include the newer hero and environment, so they are not an isolated comparison with the older 55 Hz result above. Both normal solves and the echo solve advanced real scratch-profile XP/kills/questions without page errors. However, the first attack frames jumped from about .04–.05 s to .18–.21 s, omitting much of the opening. Static acceptance does not establish a complete opening/motion pass; the first-cast stall is being investigated separately from the accepted art.

`shadow-warm-diagnostic.json` records a runtime-only same-room experiment, not a production optimization. An offscreen render prepared five additional burn/shadow programs, but the warmed first cast still had a 100 ms opening gap and a 117 ms front-release gap. The baseline had 133 ms and 100 ms gaps respectively. Steady scheduling cadence did not improve (28.3 versus 27.0 Hz). This does not justify claiming that shadow prewarming alone fixes the opening; the accepted art and production shader preparation were left unchanged.

The subsequent `actual-draw-warm-diagnostic.json` prepared the real ignition/front/contact geometry and retained its programs. The baseline created seven programs in a 120 ms first submission and six more in a 110 ms release submission. The prepared case kept 119 programs throughout, with those submissions around 5–11 ms and actual opening ages .037, .070, .103, .148 and .183 s. These submission durations are CPU-wall observations, not GPU timings. A synchronous loading-phase preparation based on this result is now implemented; fresh normal-loop validation remains pending.

`hero-comparison.json` and `cornea-comparison.json` isolate the steady rendering regression in one current world/context. Native EXT GPU timers showed the old hero at 10.55 ms median, authored hero at 25.49 ms and old-hero repeat at 11.31 ms. The authored cornea's transmission enabled a second opaque-scene pass. Changing only that material's transmission to zero reduced median GPU time from 26.57 to 12.11 ms, submissions from 319 to 181 draws, and rAF intervals from 33.3 to 16.7 ms; restoring transmission restored the cost. The rear-view silhouette retained independent 8 acceptance, while eye fidelity requires the asset agent's front-view review. These results support a material correction, not reducing the authored character geometry.


## Corrected production first-cast preparation

The final public hero uses the independently reviewed alpha-discarded cornea, preserving the iris without the costly transmission pass. Its front portrait was accepted at 8 separately from the rear-view diagnostic above.

The first loading implementation still lost its prepared erosion programs when Begin rebuilt the enemy batches. The corrected implementation retains independent material copies with the exact surface/depth shader hooks and cache keys; their lifetime is the world, not the encounter. Actual offscreen draws run synchronously behind loading, restore render targets, viewport/scissor and actor bindings, and do not change spell timing. A focused test verifies that disposing an encounter leaves these retained variants alive and preserves identical shader patches.

`retained-program-normal.json` and `authored-normal-loop.webm` record the first real answer after Begin plus another after travel. All 122 programs survive loading, Begin, both casts and their room changes. The first rendered ages include .019, .059, .067, .083, .101, .116, .137, .150, .171 and .183 seconds; the previous 100–133 ms opening gap is absent. The recorded ordinary cast averaged 54.4 Hz and a separate unrecorded cast 49.6 Hz. These are observed scheduling cadence, not GPU execution time. The 25 FPS video encoding is a separate sampling limit.

`retained-program-echo.json` and `authored-echo-loop.webm` record the real Cinderbranch burn/echo solve against 39 enemies: 32 XP, 39 kills and a new question. Programs remain at 122, the opening has rendered ages .017, .048, .067, .084, .113, .119, .140, .159 and .179 seconds, and recorded cadence averages 48.1 Hz. Both runs report no page or shader errors and confirm restoration of the normal render target and actor material/depth bindings.

Independent review of all 115 decoded frames from each corrected recording accepted the opening and complete reviewed desktop sequence at **8/10**. The compact palm gather, descending strand/near-foot seed and expanding front are now visible in order, with no skipped transition; echo integration and grounded aftermath/cleanup also remain 8. Native opening crops were inspected alongside full frames. This verdict is visual review in addition to the timing instrumentation, and retains the 40 ms recording-sampling and separate mobile/static limits stated above.
